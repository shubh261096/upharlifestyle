The four-outfit plan

Work backwards from the functions rather than forwards from the shops. This is the sequence we build with most grooms:

FunctionWhat worksWhy
Haldi / Tilak Light kurta pyjama, yellow or white, cotton or light silk It will get stained. Do not wear anything you care about, and do not wear anything heavy in the sun.
Sangeet / Mehndi Indo-western set or a bandhgala with printed trousers You need to move and dance. Structure matters less than mobility here.
Shaadi Sherwani or jodhpuri with safa, dupatta and mojari The most-photographed hours of your life. This is where the budget belongs.
Reception Three-piece suit or tuxedo, dark shades Evening, indoor, artificial light. Western formals photograph cleanest here.

Where to spend and where to save

Wedding budgets are finite, so allocate deliberately. The ceremony outfit deserves the largest share — it appears in the album more than everything else combined. The haldi kurta deserves the least; it is going to be ruined by design. Shoes are the most common false economy: mojari that cut into your feet in hour two will show on your face in every photograph taken afterwards.

Renting the ceremony sherwani and the reception tuxedo, then buying the sangeet and haldi pieces, is the split that leaves most grooms satisfied. You keep what you will wear again and you do not pay full price for two garments destined for a cupboard.

Fittings: plan two, not one

A single fitting is optimistic. The first visit sets the measurements; the second confirms the alterations landed and catches the things nobody notices on a hanger — a collar that rides up when you raise your arms, a churidar that bunches at the ankle, a dupatta that will not stay pinned. Leave a week between them. Grooms who compress this into one visit are the ones adjusting their outfit throughout the ceremony.

A short timeline

  • 8 weeks out: decide the outfit for each function, and lock colours against the bride's.
  • 6 weeks out: first fitting; confirm rentals and reserve dates.
  • 4 weeks out: choose accessories — safa, mala, kalgi, mojari — against the finished outfits.
  • 2 weeks out: second fitting, final alterations, break in the shoes.
  • 3 days out: collect everything, check each set is complete, hang it out.

Frequently asked questions

How many outfits does a dulha actually need?

Typically four: something light and washable for haldi, something you can dance in for sangeet, the main sherwani or jodhpuri for the ceremony, and a suit or tuxedo for the reception. If tilak and mehndi are separate events, add one more that you can repeat. We plan the set together so no two looks clash in the album.

Do you offer the full dulha dress on rent?

Yes. Rental makes particular sense for the ceremony sherwani and the reception tuxedo, since both are worn once. Many grooms rent those two and buy the sangeet and haldi outfits, which get reused.

When should the dulha start shopping?

Six to eight weeks before the wedding is comfortable. That leaves room for two fitting rounds and for arranging anything that is not on the floor. In peak wedding season, start earlier — the best pieces in common sizes move fast.

Can you match my outfit with the bride's lehenga?

Yes, and it is worth doing. Bring photographs of her outfits for each function. We work in the same tonal family at a different intensity, so the two of you complement each other rather than competing in the photographs.

What accessories are included?

We stock safa and pagdi, dupatta, kalgi and brooch, mala, kamarbandh and mojari. Choosing them in the same visit, under the same lighting as the main outfit, is the only reliable way to get the colours to actually match.
